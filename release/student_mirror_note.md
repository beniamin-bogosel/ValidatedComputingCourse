# Mirror project: source and scope

The [student project](../projects/mirror_trajectory.ipynb) adapts Chapter 2, *Reliability amid Chaos*, of Bornemann, Laurie, Wagon, and Waldvogel's *The SIAM 100-Digit Challenge*. The chapter spans printed pp. 33–46; §2.3 on reliable reflections is on pp. 41–43.

The original challenge uses unit speed, initial position (1/2, 1/10), an eastward direction, and circular mirrors of radius 1/3 centered at every integer lattice point. It asks for distance from the origin at time 10.

Our introductory core uses final time 3 with a stated absolute enclosure-width target. Work through approximate exploration, exact input semantics, enclosing collision calculations, event ordering, and final-time validation. Agreement at several precisions is evidence; the certificate requires the branch and coverage arguments in the project notebook.

The related [laser/butterfly-effect video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iTaSXto67WQ) is optional visual motivation supplied during planning. The course does not depend on viewing it or claim to reproduce its experimental apparatus.

The original time-10 challenge and general mirror arrangements are optional extensions. See [the rubric](../projects/README.md). Source PDFs are optional local readings and are not included in this distribution.
